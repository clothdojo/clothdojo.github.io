# ClothDojo project website

Buildless research website. GitHub Pages publishes this directory only; code, tests and benchmark fixtures remain in the same repository.

## Presentation and evidence

The homepage retains the approved centred formal title and opens with the fixed two-worktable film. Garments, re-rendered RGB data and robot transfer follow in three sliding cards, then code in a static card. The RGB count describes the open-source dataset rather than generation capacity. Sources and throughput qualifications remain in disclosures. Author names and affiliations remain at the footer; detailed experiments remain on the separate results page.

The opening film keeps two worktables and one camera fixed, cycling six garments through twelve complete model-generated task trajectories. Both tasks use the same garment identity and colour in each round. The learned NOCS-guided rollout checkpoints are recorded in the manifest; these task actions are not human demonstrations. The 65.44-second film uses 1600×560 native Isaac frames at 25 fps and 2× source replay, with CRF18 H.264 and faststart. Desktop and mobile use the same complete frame without cropping or source switching.

Task motion preserves saved initial-to-verified-successful-released states. After release, the arms return to configured home in a scripted kinematic presentation tail while the final cloth state is held. Only after both robots return home does a short dissolve introduce the next garment and trajectory pair. Home returns and dissolves are presentation rather than policy behavior. The film illustrates generated data rather than live generation throughput.

The open-source dataset contains 7,848 trajectories: 3,992 flattening and 3,856 folding, covering 400 and 399 garments respectively. These are dataset counts, not a limit on generation capacity. NOCS-conditioned privileged policies generate complete task trajectories autonomously; recorded motion is subsequently re-rendered into RGB observations. The 500 garments are a core pool for generation and evaluation, not 500 manually demonstrated garments. Human supervision covers only part of the garment space and can include assets outside the core pool. Privileged policies generate on garments absent from their human supervision; held-out evaluation depends on the task and protocol. Generator-supervision and downstream RGB-training splits must not be conflated or shown as one universal 400/100 partition. The atlas shows geometry/material diversity rather than split membership; the opening film selects six garments from the generated data.

`assets/transfer73/` shows five complete worktables simultaneously: ALOHA 2 and X5 in the back row, PIPER, Franka and UR5e in the front. The shared camera is fitted during native RTX rendering without post-cropping. Original cloth and robot states are replayed at 2× speed and 25 fps, followed by a scripted home return and a 0.48-second loop dissolve. This is a saved PIPER demonstration with four kinematic retargets, not five-robot closed-loop policy evaluation. Ground normalization preserves contact shadows on white. The RGB card separately presents scene/appearance re-rendering with fixed Flattening/Folding labels.

The model comparison reports the same four garment–appearance conditions for all four models. π₀.₅, GR00T N1.7 and the internal StarVLA-based policy are fine-tuned; Diffusion Policy is trained from scratch. Pretraining differences are included, so these results are not an architecture-only ablation.

## Shared research presentation

All homepage and experiment-page charts are HTML components from `results.css`, with a common 0–100% success scale. Flattening is blue-green; folding is brown. The data source or model is identified by its row label, not a different chart style.

All research tables use `.table-block` with a caption outside the horizontally scrolling `.table-wrap > .data-table`. They share typography, numeric alignment, headings and borders; narrow screens show a scrolling hint for wide tables. Captions remain fully readable while numeric columns scroll. Full numerical results and experimental qualifications are retained. Combined data does not exceed Generated alone; three epochs do not mean equal optimizer steps; pretrained models are distinguished from Diffusion Policy trained from scratch.

The first usage entry is an executable CPU saved-state scoring example. Generation, training and full evaluation workflows disclose their dependence on forthcoming model/data/visual-resource bundles.

## Delivery and playback

Web videos use H.264 with `yuv420p` and fast-start metadata. They preserve the rendered aspect ratio. Only the selected variation is attached to its player. The shared player policy permits one playing video at a time, pauses offscreen/hidden media, and suppresses autoplay for reduced-motion or data-saver preferences. A manually paused visible video remains paused until it leaves view or the visitor chooses another item.

Videos referenced by the current homepage, results and explorer (including catalog clips and hover previews) run at 25 fps. Frames sample saved physical states or the original RGB recordings; low-rate video is not repeated or interpolated to manufacture a higher motion frame rate. Completed generation trajectories retain their verified terminal state, and short expert demonstrations retain their final frame until the shared phase boundary at 29.64 seconds. Archived design-study media are outside the current page set.

Homepage videos have no native controls or play/pause buttons. They play automatically in view and loop. The opening film has no chapter buttons or expanded player. The shared ambient playback policy still pauses offscreen videos and permits only one active video.

The homepage keeps the opening two-worktable film above three horizontal highlights: garments, re-rendered RGB observations and scene/robot transfer. The Chrome review captures all three sliding cards and the static code card at desktop/mobile widths and exercises arrows, dots, mouse dragging, keyboard navigation, deep links and reduced-motion playback alongside the opening-film checks.

Current homepage checks run in `.github/scripts/homepage-film-review.cjs`: both public homepage variants across desktop, short-wide windows and mobile, complete title and first-screen fitting, white ground, no hero controls, reduced motion, a full six-case loop on desktop/mobile and exclusive offscreen-aware playback. Generator provenance is checked against the original collection metadata. Historical study checks remain in the other review scripts.

The complete cloth animations stream in independent 50-frame chunks. Initial shared geometry, poses, full cloth geometry, the first two seconds and unchanged cotton textures download in parallel. The lighting page skips duplicate low-LOD animations for its two full-resolution focus garments. Background animation has priority over distant workcells until the complete loop is buffered; the clock cannot advance into unavailable samples. Geometry and quantized positions are identical to the prior whole-file animation. `firstFrameMs` and buffered frame counts are diagnostic state. Browser startup checks deliberately block later animation and distant workcells and verify that a complete-quality first frame is already rendered; timings from software WebGL are not target-device benchmarks.

The live camera interpolates target and distance linearly between calibrated views. The pullback regression evaluates the actual camera and visibility functions at 1001 positions in Chrome without repeatedly drawing the heavy scene, checking every workcell's opacity never decreases as the camera retreats. Rendered near/far screenshots and continuous input checks validate the visible result. Reveal margins also account for short, wide windows. The lighting study raises cloth display lightness and chroma in sRGB while preserving hue, then converts the adjusted identity color back to linear albedo; the full cotton tint uses the same adjustment. The roughness, normal maps, specular parameters and scene exposure are unchanged. These are presentation colors, not paper appearance randomization.

`lighting-study.html` compares the restored-cloth scene under the previous lighting and a PMREM room environment, fitted VSM shadows and near-field SSAO. Official Three.js r180 `RoomEnvironment`, `SSAOPass` and their direct imports are vendored under the existing MIT license. The environment illuminates surfaces without displaying a background room. SSAO uses 12 samples at half CSS resolution, fading out with camera distance; the far view skips it. Both modes share geometry, materials and action time. This is a real-time approximation, not RTX indirect lighting. `.github/scripts/lighting-study-review.cjs` captures matched near frames and far views, and verifies camera/lighting interaction without additional network requests.

`cloth-study.html` compares the two opening garments using their complete source triangle meshes and the original cotton base-color, normal and roughness maps against the previous simplified cloth, sharing the same action time, camera, robot and lights. All source triangles and face-varying UVs are retained; UV seams duplicate some display vertices. Textures are resized to at most 1024 pixels with source tint/roughness transforms retained. This restores source simulation geometry, not offline subdivision or RTX lighting. Assets and precision are recorded in `assets/cloth48/manifest.json`. Both variants load before comparison is enabled; switching and dragging make no requests. Paused camera movement does not recompute cloth positions/normals. `.github/scripts/cloth-study-review.cjs` captures matched frames and exercises continuous dragging on desktop and mobile. Software WebGL timings do not measure target-device GPU performance.

`.github/workflows/website-visual-review.yml` uses Playwright 1.56.1 and the runner's installed Google Chrome for H.264 playback. It accepts a Git ref and page path, and reviews the website work branches before publication on `main`.
